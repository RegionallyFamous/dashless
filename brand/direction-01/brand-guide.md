# Dashless — identity direction 01

**Creative direction: rebellious software. Bold, playful, anti-dashboard.**

Status: first identity proposal for discussion. The board is an AI-generated concept, not final logo artwork. This package defines a complete candidate identity; it does not change the released product or its default publication theme.

## The idea

Dashless gives independent publishers a way out of daily dashboard work while keeping the WordPress foundation they own. The attitude is mischievous. The editorial process is careful. The writer stays in charge.

**Primary descriptor:** WordPress without the dashboard.

**Campaign line:** Less admin. More you.

**Ownership line:** Your words. Your web.

Use the descriptor at first contact. Give each application one lead line rather than stacking all three as competing slogans.

## Positioning

For independent writers, small publications, and WordPress people who want to spend more time making their site their own, Dashless is a conversational publishing workflow that turns WordPress into the foundation rather than the daily workspace. Local Codex handles editorial work; WordPress retains content and revisions; Astro presents the publication. Writers review the actual site and approve a specific version before publication.

The emotional benefit is authorship: this is your corner of the web, and running it should feel like making something. The practical benefit is working through conversation with concrete previews and publication controls. Avoid promising measured time savings until they have been measured.

Primary audience: independent publishers already comfortable with WordPress and willing to use Codex. Secondary audience: developers and designers helping them. The first release is not a universal hosted service for every nontechnical user; setup requirements belong in the path to installation.

The opponent is unnecessary admin busywork. Never make WordPress users, less technical writers, or accessibility needs the butt of the joke. Dashless depends on WordPress and is proud of that relationship.

## Brand character

- **Defiant:** question the assumption that publishing requires a dashboard.
- **Playful:** a bear walks out of a software window carrying a pencil.
- **Plainspoken:** say what will happen and what the writer needs to do.
- **Dependable:** a bold campaign is supported by explicit drafts, previews, approval, and rollback behavior.

Three proof pillars:

1. **Work in conversation.** Ask for a draft, revise it, and manage existing content from local Codex.
2. **See what you will publish.** Review the actual Astro page; publication is tied to the approved revision.
3. **Keep your foundation.** WordPress stores editorial content. The Astro source remains yours. Dashless has no proprietary publishing cloud.

## Naming and message hierarchy

Write **Dashless** in prose. The visual wordmark is **dashless**, lowercase. Use no hyphen and no camel case. Introduce it as “Dashless, a conversational publishing workflow for WordPress.” Avoid relying on the term “AI CMS” to explain it.

Use WordPress, Codex, Astro, and WP Cloud as factual product references, never as parts of the Dashless logo or as implied endorsements. Treat the publishing stack as explanatory detail after the benefit.

Suggested functional vocabulary:

- Draft: unpublished editorial work.
- Preview: the actual page to review.
- Publish: make the approved version public.
- Site: the publisher’s own website.
- Revision: a specific saved content version.

Keep familiar functional names. Do not turn releases into “escapes,” failures into “rebellions,” or the workflow into a collection of mascot puns.

## Voice

Short, concrete sentences. Active verbs. A little nerve. One joke at a time. Contractions are welcome. Campaign headlines can be huge and all caps; documentation and product messages use normal sentence case.

| Context | Candidate copy | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First impression | WordPress without the dashboard. | Explain immediately. |
| Campaign | Less admin. More you. | Lead with the writer’s benefit. |
| Landing headline | Leave the dashboard behind. | Make a direct invitation. |
| Supporting copy | Draft in conversation. Preview your site. Publish the version you approved. | Ground the attitude in behavior. |
| Ownership | Your words. Your web. | Pair with concrete ownership facts. |
| Draft saved | Draft saved in WordPress. Ready to preview. | State exactly what happened. |
| Preview ready | Your preview is ready. Review it before publishing. | Give a clear next step. |
| Stale preview | This post changed after the preview was created. Generate a new preview before publishing. | Explain the blocker without blame. |
| Verified success | Published. Your approved version is live. | Only after public verification succeeds. |
| Failed activation with verified rollback | The new release could not be activated. The previous version has been restored and verified. | Only say this when both restoration and verification are confirmed. |
| Setup | Connect your WordPress site to get started. | Say what the user needs to do. |

Avoid “magic,” “effortless,” “revolutionary,” “AI-powered everything,” “never log in again,” and “publish instantly.” Do not imply that approval is optional or that administration no longer exists. The dashboard remains available for accounts, plugins, and emergencies.

Use the sharpest lines in campaigns, not failure messages. No jokes during credentials, irreversible actions, publication failures, or recovery.

## Visual concept: break out of the frame

The recurring device is a software-window frame that opens or breaks at one edge. It means leaving the admin interface to make something. Use one interruption per composition: a word crosses a rule, an illustration exits a frame, or a label tilts slightly. The underlying layout remains disciplined.

Do not rebuild a dense fake dashboard in the marketing. Decorative window chrome is for a specific escape illustration, not the container for every section. Avoid crossed-out content names in final artwork: crossing out “Posts” and “Pages” can imply the content is being removed. The concept board shows this rough idea; production art should replace it with abstract menu strokes or the word “Admin.”

## Logo system

Primary direction: heavy lowercase sans lettering with tight optical spacing, gently softened corners, and slight forward movement. The generated board is the visual reference. The guide’s live wordmark is a typeset approximation, not the master logo.

Required final lockups:

- Primary wordmark in Ink on Paper.
- Reversed wordmark in Paper on Ink.
- One-color black or white version.
- Wordmark with descriptor for first encounters.
- Small mark based on the final custom lowercase “d,” for an avatar and favicon only after small-size testing.

Clear space: at least half the wordmark’s lowercase letter height on all sides. Provisional minimum width: 120 CSS px on screen or 30 mm in print; validate after vector drawing. Omit the descriptor when it would render below 14 CSS px. The proposed small mark needs dedicated 16, 24, and 32 px checks.

Never use a hyphen as the primary symbol: it weakens the dashboard meaning and turns the name into a punctuation joke. Do not stretch the lettering, add fake 3D depth, place texture inside small logos, or use the full mascot as a favicon. The generated lettering must be redrawn and optically refined before production SVG/PDF export.

## Color

| Token | Value | Role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ink | #24212B | Main text, wordmark, dark surfaces |
| Paper | #F7F2E8 | Primary light surface |
| Acid | #DFFF00 | Signature campaign fill, primary CTA fill, active accent |
| Vermilion | #C6402B | Editorial correction marks, limited emphasis |
| Lilac | #C7B9F2 | Secondary panels and quieter editorial accents |
| Apricot | #E8AC71 | Bear fur; primarily illustration |
| Muted | #625D68 | Secondary text on Paper |

Typical composition: about 70% Paper/Ink, 20% Acid, and 10% secondary color. Campaign posters may use a full Acid field. Use at most one secondary accent per small application.

Use Ink text on Paper, Acid, Lilac, and Apricot. Use Paper text on Ink or Vermilion. Do not use Acid text on Paper or Paper text on Acid. Color never carries the entire meaning of a status. Include a label or icon. The board is raster concept art; these exact token values govern implementations.

## Typography

Direction: a very heavy grotesk for short headlines, a neutral readable sans for body text, and restrained monospace for labels and technical annotations.

The local prototype uses Arial Black for display, the platform system sans for body, and a system monospace stack for labels. It loads no external fonts. These are practical prototype choices; the custom logo is a separate drawing. Do not distribute local system-font files with the brand.

- Campaign display: 64–144 px desktop; 44–64 px mobile; line height about 0.95–1.0. Use 2–5 words per line and deliberate breaks.
- Section heading: 32–48 px; line height 1.05–1.15.
- Body: 17–20 px; line height 1.5–1.65; around 55–70 characters per line.
- Labels: 11–13 px monospace, uppercase sparingly. Never set long prose this way.
- Buttons: 15–17 px bold, descriptive text, at least 44 px tall.

Keep body type upright and comfortably spaced. Do not make the entire UI condensed, italic, or uppercase just because the logo is loud.

## Mascot: the editor bear

Keep the existing teddy connection, give it agency. Working label: **the Dashless bear**. A separate proper name is unnecessary at this stage.

Appearance: compact apricot teddy, round ears, oval eyes, cream muzzle, broad paws, thick dark outlines, one confident raised eyebrow and a small smile. Carry an editorial pencil or a page. The bear is the publisher’s accomplice, not a substitute author.

Signature scene: stepping out of a window frame with a pencil. Supporting future poses: reviewing a page, pointing toward a preview, resting beside a finished publication. Preserve face proportions and recognizable silhouette across poses. Every pose must have coherent paws and limbs.

Use the bear in onboarding, campaign art, release notes, and occasional empty states. Keep it out of publication approval controls and serious error states. No endless costume variants, robot eyes, floating AI sparkles, or mascot chatter in every interaction. At small sizes use the wordmark or future “d” mark.

## Layout, imagery, and motion

Use broad whitespace, large type, a small number of solid panels, and 2 px rules. Favor square corners or a 4 px radius. Offset shadow is optional for a sticker, not a default on every card. Slight rotations belong to a single decorative label, not body copy or buttons.

Illustrations use two or three flat colors and robust outlines. Print texture is optional and restrained, never placed over small type. Photography, if later introduced, should depict real editorial work or personal sites rather than anonymous laptop stock scenes.

Motion idea: a frame slides away to reveal the page. Use a brief 160–240 ms transition, once on an intentional interaction. No autoplay typewriter effects, glitch text, or flashing. Reduced-motion mode is static. Ensure keyboard focus is visible on every background with an offset contrasting ring.

## Applications

### Landing page

Navigation: How it works / Your site / Docs.

Eyebrow: FOR PEOPLE WHO STILL LOVE WORDPRESS.

Headline: **Leave the dashboard behind.**

Body: Draft and revise in conversation. Preview your real site. Publish the version you approved. Dashless brings local Codex, WordPress, and Astro into one publishing workflow you control.

Primary CTA: **Get started** → setup documentation. Secondary CTA: **See how it works** → a real walkthrough.

Proof strip: Local workflow / WordPress content / Your Astro source.

Early workflow section: Draft → Preview → Approve → Publish. Give each step a short concrete explanation. Explain the Codex, Node, HTTPS WordPress, and host requirements before installation.

### README and repository

Use the descriptor directly below the wordmark. Show one useful workflow image or the signature bear illustration, then a concise explanation and a direct setup link. Replace inflated claims with behavioral proof. The existing README already supplies the product story; these are candidate editorial changes, not an automatic rewrite.

### Social and campaign

1200 × 630 share card: wordmark top-left; “Less admin. More you.” in oversized Ink on Acid; descriptor in the lower band. Respect a 60 px inset. No fake UI controls on a share card.

Square campaign: “The dashboard is optional.” plus the escaping bear. Keep one headline, one brand signature, one clear idea. Stickers: wordmark on Ink/Paper or the bear illustration with a generous cut line. Print artwork requires dedicated production exports and proofs.

### Product behavior

Express the identity through concise copy, generous spacing, Acid for a primary action, and exact state descriptions. Preview and publication always remain distinct. A playful visual identity must not make publishing look casual or automatic.

### Publisher sites

Dashless is the tool’s identity. The publisher’s name, voice, and visual identity belong to their site. Do not force Acid, the bear, or a Dashless wordmark onto every generated publication. Hypertext Diary can remain a separate default theme. No mandatory “Made with Dashless” badge.

## Candidate launch copy

**Short bio:** WordPress without the dashboard. Draft in conversation, preview your site, and publish the version you approved.

**Launch post:** Still love WordPress? You can leave the dashboard behind. Dashless brings drafting and revision into local Codex, keeps your content in WordPress, and builds your public site with Astro. Review the real page. Approve the version you want. Publish. Your words. Your web.

**One-line pitch:** Dashless lets you publish to WordPress through conversation, with a real site preview and your approval before anything goes live.
